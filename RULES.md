# My Ethical Hacking Ground Rules

These rules govern everything I do in this repository and in my cybersecurity practice.
I read them at the start of every week.

## The one rule that matters most
I only ever scan, probe, test, or exploit systems that I own, or that I have
**explicit written permission** to test. No exceptions.

## Why this matters
Unauthorized access to computer systems is a crime — under laws such as the
US Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA), the UK Computer Misuse Act, and
equivalent laws in other countries, including Nigeria's Cybercrimes Act.
The skills I am learning are powerful and are legal *only* when used with
authorization. Authorization is the entire difference between an ethical
hacker who gets hired and a criminal who gets prosecuted.

## Where I practice
I practice exclusively in legal, sandboxed environments:
- My own virtual machines and my own WSL2 Ubuntu setup
- TryHackMe
- Hack The Box
- PortSwigger Web Security Academy
- OverTheWire

When I use bug bounty platforms (e.g. HackerOne, Bugcrowd) in the future,
I will stay strictly within each program's published scope — that scope is
my written permission.

## Responsible disclosure
If I ever discover a real vulnerability in a system I am authorized to test,
I will report it privately and responsibly to the owner, give them reasonable
time to fix it, and never exploit it for personal gain or publicize it
prematurely.

## Repository hygiene
I will never commit passwords, API keys, private credentials, or details of
real targets to this repository.
